# Paul campaign: from persecutor to witness

Campaign design draft, 6 November 2026.
Status: narrative and gameplay blueprint, not an implemented campaign.
The existing Damascus scene is a technical prototype for Act III, not the campaign opening.

## Player promise

Experience Paul's transformation through first-person exploration, relationships and directed scenes.
Cover his documented public journey and a clearly identified traditional epilogue, not every year of his life.
Player choices affect exploration and understanding, not the established historical outcomes.
The campaign opens before the Damascus encounter and closes in Rome.

## Five-act journey

| Act | Playable episode proposals | Player actions | Learning outcome | Source anchors |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I. Saul before the encounter | Jerusalem; Stephen's witness; preparations for Damascus | Explore a restrained courtyard, listen to contrasting accounts, inspect the travel authorization and leave for Damascus. Stephen's death is contextual, not a combat challenge. | Understand Saul's convictions and responsibility for persecution without justifying it. | Acts 7:58–8:3; 9:1–2; Paul's retrospective account in Galatians 1:13–14. |
| II. The road to Damascus | The encounter; entry into the city; Ananias | Walk the road; experience a directed encounter; accept guidance during blindness; meet Ananias. | Recognize conversion as an encounter and dependence on others, not merely a name change. | Acts 9:3–19; compare Acts 22:6–16 and 26:12–18 before final scripting. |
| III. A changed life | Damascus preaching; basket escape; Jerusalem and Barnabas | Meet disciples, hear reactions, cooperate in the escape, then face distrust and receive Barnabas's help in Jerusalem. | Understand that conversion does not instantly erase other people's fear or eliminate danger. | Acts 9:19–30; compare Galatians 1:15–24 and 2 Corinthians 11:32–33 before fixing transitions. |
| IV. The mission | Antioch and departure; Philippi; Athens; community life | Prepare journeys, explore a household and prison, listen and address an audience, work alongside a community. Select representative episodes, not a checklist of every city. | Discover mission as shared work across communities, including conflict, service and perseverance. | Candidate anchors: Acts 13; 15; 16; 17:16–34; 18:1–4; 20. These episodes require focused source review before implementation. |
| V. Imprisonment and final witness | Arrest and appeal; sea journey; Rome; traditional epilogue | Explore confinement, receive visitors, participate in a bounded shipboard sequence and hear the final reflection. | Distinguish what Acts narrates from later testimony about Paul's death. | Candidate anchors: Acts 21–28. Acts ends with Paul receiving visitors and proclaiming in Rome; the martyrdom epilogue uses explicitly identified Christian tradition. |

Act IV's selection is provisional. Do not imply that these four episodes exhaust Paul's mission.
Do not give every act the same duration or repeat the same object-search mechanic.

## Historical and pastoral rules

1. Each episode has a source sheet: passage, facts retained, invented staging, disputed chronology and reviewer status.
2. Label content as Scripture, later Christian tradition, or gameplay reconstruction.
3. No invented dialogue is displayed as a quotation. Do not copy a modern Spanish Bible translation without checking reproduction conditions.
4. Do not imply Saul changed his name to Paul at conversion; Acts 13:9 introduces the parallel name later.
5. Do not insert a horse, exact date, specific route or reconstructed architecture as an attested fact without evidence.
6. Saul's actions must not become a reward loop for finding, arresting or harming Christians. Do not award persecution points.
7. Do not portray Jewish identity or Jewish people collectively as the antagonist. Situate individuals and conflicts in their specific contexts.
8. No miracle is a player-triggered supernatural power or a skill test. Directed scenes preserve the source's agency.
9. No invented internal monologue claims to disclose Paul's actual thoughts.
10. Obtain Catholic/historical review of the chapter scripts before calling them historically validated.

The first source pass checked Acts 7 and 9 and the Vatican catechesis on martyrdom.
The remaining anchors above are research tasks, not completed verification.
Do not flatten the intervals in Acts or reconcile Paul's letters and Acts into a confident invented chronology.

## First playable chapter: Jerusalem, before departure

This is the next implementation slice, not a claim that the current prototype already contains it.

- Entry: a small courtyard connected to a street and departure point; no open-world Jerusalem.
- Dramatic question: what convictions lead Saul to seek the Damascus authorization, and who is affected?
- Beat 1: explore the courtyard and hear a short conversation establishing the conflict.
- Beat 2: encounter a personal consequence of persecution through a clearly labeled reconstructed scene.
- Beat 3: inspect the authorization and understand Saul's stated purpose from Acts 9:1–2.
- Beat 4: leave toward Damascus; conclude at the threshold of Act II.
- Stephen's death may be introduced through restrained contextual staging; no graphic violence or player-operated execution.
- NPCs require readable presence and intentional dialogue timing; a letter and colored boxes alone are not sufficient final presentation.
- The player can inspect details in any order, but contextual narrative gates keep the departure understandable.
- Camera control is captured on desktop; Escape pauses and restores the cursor. Touch controls remain supported.
- Dialogue presentation pauses exploration input and resumes it deliberately.
- Restart returns to a coherent chapter opening. Do not promise persistence until implemented and tested.
- Source panel names what is attested and what was reconstructed.

Acceptance checks for the future implementation:
- Complete courtyard → consequence → authorization → departure without instruction from a developer.
- Cannot leave before the required context is delivered, but no irreversible soft-lock results from an incorrect interaction order.
- No interaction offers points or success feedback for persecution.
- Pause, pointer-lock loss, dialogue close and restart restore the correct input state.
- Keyboard and touch expose the same required interactions.
- A player can explain who Saul is before Damascus and why he is traveling.
- Human testing checks learning and tone; automated tests cannot establish either.

## Existing prototype and production order

1. Keep the existing Damascus scene available as a technical test while building Act I.
2. Implement the first Jerusalem chapter with placeholder assets, explicitly described as a blockout.
3. Test movement, dialogue and the narrative transition; then produce approved visual/audio references.
4. Build Act II and connect it to Act III. Do not jump directly from conversion to the escape without a reviewed transition.
5. Expand mission episodes after validating the reusable scene, dialogue and chapter-transition systems.
6. Build Rome and the separately labeled traditional epilogue.
7. Review the campaign for learning, source distinctions and continuity.

Do not market the roadmap as five implemented chapters. Do not turn chapter menus into a substitute for playable episodes.
Do not redesign the engine or add a backend merely to represent the campaign outline.
